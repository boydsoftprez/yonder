Three cameras on one aircraft

This is where "one class of camera view" earns its keep. In all three, every camera page is the same component with a different camera bound to it — what differs is how you get between them, and what that costs over a cellular link.

What R‑UI‑03 already asks for, plus the thing it doesn't cover. Navigation is built from detected hardware — three cameras, three entries; unplug one and its entry is gone — and each page deep‑links, so a bookmark to the gimbal is a bookmark to the gimbal. The strip is the addition: one tap to switch, and you can see what the others see without paying for three live streams. Only the camera you are looking at is subscribed over WebRTC; the other two are stills refreshed every few seconds, which is a few kilobits rather than a few megabits. The strip also doubles as the fallback for a browser that cannot establish WebRTC at all — a still every few seconds is a great deal better than a black rectangle.

One nav entry, and the switcher is in the page. Tidier navigation on a six‑camera aircraft. But the selector is a tab strip, so R‑UI‑12 requires the capture gate to photograph the page once per camera anyway, and you have lost the deep link — "open the gimbal" becomes "open Cameras, then press Gimbal". And you cannot see what the others see without leaving the one you are on.

Everything visible, and it triples your cellular bill. Three live WebRTC subscriptions to see two pictures nobody is looking at closely — 5.4 Mb/s where option 1 spends 1.9. It is also ambiguous in a way the others are not: every control on the page has to say which camera it belongs to, which is the beginning of the confusion we are trying to remove. Worth keeping in mind as something the Cockpit might want later, on a link that can afford it — not as the way this page works.

The same page, bound to a camera that cannot move

Nothing here is a second page or a second template. The belly camera is a fixed CSI sensor with no gimbal, no zoom and no white‑balance control, so the Aim group is not there and the deck is two columns instead of three. Recentre is gone from the rail; the gesture on the picture does nothing and says so. This is what "one class of camera view" actually buys — the page is generated from what the camera reports it can do, not from what kind of camera somebody decided it was.
