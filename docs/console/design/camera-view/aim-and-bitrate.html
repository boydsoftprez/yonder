Aiming, and choosing a bitrate

Both mockups carry the same new pieces — the dial in the Aim group, and bitrate as a picker rather than a slider. What differs is only what a drag on the picture means, and that is the one decision here, because it is the difference between a gimbal that is usable over a cellular link and one that is not.

How every real gimbal controller works, and the only one that survives a cellular link. How far you push is how fast it slews; let go and it stops. You are never chasing a position you cannot see yet, so 300 ms of lag makes the gimbal feel slow rather than making it uncontrollable. The dial is the same control for a mouse — self‑centring, with the white pointer showing where the camera actually is and the cyan puck showing what you are asking for, so the lag is visible instead of being something you fight.

One thing this forces us to write down. A held rate command has to repeat, and it has to expire — if your finger lifts, or the link drops mid‑slew, the commands simply stop arriving and the gimbal stops because nothing is telling it to move. That is the absence of a command, not Yonder deciding to stop, and it stays the right side of rule 4. Anything that recentres itself on link loss would be Yonder making a decision, and we should refuse to build it however reasonable it sounds.

Immediately obvious, and it fights you at range. Tapping a spot and having the camera go there is the nicest thing on this page when the link is 20 ms. At 300 ms you are aiming at a picture that is already stale, you overshoot, you correct against a picture that is stale again, and you oscillate — the failure R‑UI‑06 exists to prevent.

It is not worthless: tap‑to‑point is the right gesture for "look at that thing over there", because it is one command rather than a stream of them, and stale-by-300 ms barely matters when the move is large. My suggestion is that we take this as a second gesture on top of A rather than as the primary one — drag to slew, tap to send it somewhere.

Bitrate, when you switch it to Adaptive

Same control, second state. Fixed gives you one picker; adaptive gives you a floor and a ceiling, and the bar stops being an input and becomes the readout of what is actually leaving the aircraft (R‑VID‑07, R‑VID‑08).

Also changed here, and worth a word: resolution is a picker of what this camera actually reports, not a fixed list. A USB camera enumerates the modes it has; offering 4K on a 1080p sensor is a bug waiting to be filed, and the same list is what lets us refuse a combination the board cannot sustain before you press anything (R‑CAM‑10).
