When the link goes, and when it comes back

Nothing on the aircraft changes. The pipeline's state is whatever you last set it to, and losing your console link is not an instruction — under rule 4 it cannot be one. The browser's WebRTC session ends because nobody is subscribed, which is the protocol doing its job rather than Yonder deciding; the RTP push to the ground station is unaffected, and your losing contact says nothing at all about their link. So the only real question is what the page does, and the danger is a stale picture an operator reads as a live one.

The picture never disappears, and it never pretends. It desaturates, it darkens, it takes a hatch, and it carries an age that keeps counting — three signals at once, because a badge alone is the thing an operator stops seeing after ten minutes. By state 4 the shot is barely readable and the count is the loudest thing on it, which is right: at that point its only value is telling you what you were looking at when contact went.

The line I care most about is in the bottom-right corner. Ground station: not known from here. Your console losing its link tells you nothing whatever about the aircraft's other outputs, and a page that greys out the ground-station row — or worse, keeps it green — is inventing a fact. Saying plainly that we cannot know is the only honest state, and it is the one that a page assembled from live readbacks rather than from remembered values gets for free.

Impossible to misread, and it throws away the one thing you still had. There is no chance of mistaking this for a live picture. But the last frame before contact went is often exactly what you want — where was it pointing, what was in shot, was the horizon where it should be — and going black deletes it to solve a problem that desaturation, a hatch and a running count already solve.

Worth saying about coming back: the page must not restore itself from what it remembers sending. R‑CTL‑10 says settings are read back rather than taken from form defaults, and a reconnection is the exact moment that matters — the aircraft may have rebooted, K‑41 says the dev board does, and a console that redraws its old values would show you a configuration nobody is running.
