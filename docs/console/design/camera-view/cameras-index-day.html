One page that answers "what does this aircraft have, and what did it refuse". Each row goes to that camera's page; each row also says in one line what the capability probe actually got back, so aim: none on the nose camera explains why its page has no Aim group before you go looking. The two rejections are the ones you would really hit on your own bench today — a camera that only offers raw, and the board's broken JPEG decoder, which looks like a camera to everything that asks and will be tried once by every person who ever sees it.

The board and uplink totals belong here rather than on a camera's page, because both are shared: starting the gimbal would need 2.1 more is a sentence no single camera's page can say.

And the same picture in both palettes

The layout does not change between them — that is ADR‑0009's rule and it holds here. What does change is the video itself: a 400‑nit rectangle in the middle of a night display undoes the dark adaptation the whole palette exists to protect, so the picture carries its own dimming, on by default at night and off by day, with the level on the pane rather than buried in settings. The instruments around it are the palette's job; the video is not, because it is a photograph and no stylesheet reaches inside it.
