Four receivers, one set of facts. The segment picks who is receiving; the block underneath is generated, never stored. QGroundControl gets a settings recipe rather than a command — Video Source: UDP h.264, Port 5604 — because that is what its dialog wants; Mission Planner gets a pipeline ending in appsink, because that is what its box wants. Same three facts, four renderings.

Copy is on the rail, not beside the code block. ADR‑0009 is absolute that no action lives anywhere else on a page, and a copy button floating in the corner of a panel is exactly the small exception that ends the rule. It also means Copy works the same on a tablet where there is no cursor to hover with.

The address line is the part I would not have thought to draw a week ago. A board on a mesh has several addresses and only one of them is the one you are actually reaching it on — so the command carries that one, and lists the others underneath rather than guessing.

What the component brings, and what the Cockpit brings. The picture, the drag‑to‑slew gesture, the pinch, the camera strip and the degrade‑and‑age behaviour are all the same code as the camera page — that is the point of building it embeddable. The tapes, the annunciators, the facts block and the map are the Cockpit's, and they sit over the video because this is the flying view and the picture is the subject. That is deliberately the opposite of what I recommended for the camera page, where a panel covering the shot was the reason to reject a layout: there, the picture is what you are adjusting; here, it is what you are flying by.

Nothing on this rail changes a configuration. Swap, zoom, recentre, record — every one of them is a live command with an immediate readback, and there is no Apply, because a page you fly with is not a page you configure on. Camera page is the way out to the deck. That is also what keeps R‑UI‑10's "one primary action per page" honest here: the Cockpit's primary action is not on this rail at all.

Two things I would flag before this gets built. The strip still shows the other cameras as stills, so the Cockpit costs the same uplink as one camera page rather than three — but a pilot who wants two pictures at once will ask, and that is the moment to reconsider the mosaic I set aside earlier. And the aim crosshair sits at the centre of the video, which is not the centre of the display once the tapes are on; worth checking on a board that the offset does not read as a boresight error.
