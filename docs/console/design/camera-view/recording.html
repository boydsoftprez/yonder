Recording is a capability like every other one, and its destination is part of the answer. This camera has its own card and records 4K/60 to it while handing the board 720p — so recording anywhere else would throw away the footage you actually want. A plain USB camera has no card, so its recording is a branch off the tee onto the board's card, at whatever the pipeline is producing. Same key, same group, same readouts; the Where row says which, and the remaining-time bar is whichever card is doing the work.

Note that Yonder never sees the file when the camera records. That means no listing, no download, no delete — which is honest, and worth saying on the page rather than letting someone hunt for a file browser that cannot exist.

Uniform, and Yonder owns the files — it can list, download and delete them the way R‑SYS‑06 already does for flight logs. But on this camera it records the 720p live view and leaves the 4K the camera could have made, which is the wrong footage. It also puts a sustained write on the boot card during flight, which R‑STO‑02 and R‑STO‑05 have opinions about.

The most capable, and the one that adds a decision to every flight. Worth having eventually — Both is genuinely useful, since the board copy survives losing the camera. But it is a setup field on a page whose whole point is that it does not make you choose things the hardware has already decided, so I would add it when somebody asks rather than now.

The four states that group has to show

The third one is the reason this needs an annunciator rather than a label. The bench found that this camera acknowledges a record command whether or not a card is in it — an acknowledgement means the frame was well formed, not that anything happened. A page that turned green on the acknowledgement would be lying, which is exactly what R‑UI‑05 is about.

Three other things on this page came out of the bench note rather than out of me, and I have drawn them in rather than describing them: the group headers now say whether a control applies live or restarts the picture; a control the camera advertises but will not answer is shown, marked, and inoperative rather than hidden — that is the live-view resolution here; and the dial carries the travel the gimbal reports and the stop it is against, with the limit annunciated over the picture, because the limit flag arrives twenty times a second on the same link as the video and is not something to infer from the shot.
