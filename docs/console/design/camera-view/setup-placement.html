Where does what this camera is get set?

Layout B, developed. Two different kinds of setting want to live on this page and they behave differently: what the camera is doing right now — resolution, bitrate, zoom, exposure — takes effect immediately and reads back from the camera; what the camera is — its source, its device, its encoder, where its outputs go — is a write to config.yaml that goes through the apply engine. Three ways to seat the second kind.

Setup is done once and never in flight; the live controls are used constantly. Two soft keys — Live and Setup — swap the deck under the picture, and the picture, the readout strip and the rail stay exactly where they are. This is not the objection I raised against overlaying controls on the video: nothing covers the shot, you simply change which set of controls is under it. The cost is one more page state for the build-time capture gate to photograph in each palette, which is precisely what R‑UI‑12 was written to handle.

The cleanest live page of the three, and the one that splits the thing you asked to unify. A Cameras setup page would list every camera and its plumbing in one table, which is genuinely a good way to see a three‑camera aircraft at once. But you would be setting a camera up on one page and finding out whether it worked on another — which is the exact fault we started this conversation by naming.

Note the soft‑key rail changed: Apply is now the filled select‑tone key, not magenta. A camera change cannot cost you the ability to reach the device, and the apply engine already skips the confirmation countdown for changes that cannot — so nothing here takes the page away, and nothing here earns the irreversible tone.
