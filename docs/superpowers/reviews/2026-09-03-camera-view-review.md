# The camera view — review

**Reviews:** [`specs/2026-09-03-camera-view-design.md`](../specs/2026-09-03-camera-view-design.md) · **Date:** 2026-09-03

A multi-agent review of the camera view design, run after the design was agreed and
committed. Sixteen independent reviewers read the spec against the requirements, the ADRs,
both hardware notes, the known issues and the shipped code — ten hunting defects, six
proposing enhancements. Findings were merged across reviewers, and **every one was then
attacked by three separate verifiers instructed to refute by default**, each from a
different angle: is it true of the documents, is it already handled somewhere the finder
did not read, and does it actually matter. Majority refutation killed it.

**106 findings raised, 51 refuted, 50 confirmed. 65 proposals filtered to 22.** The
refutation rate is the number worth knowing: roughly half of what a confident reviewer
produces about this document does not survive being checked.

# Camera view design — review

Read against the spec, `requirements.md`, `roadmap.md`, `known-issues.md`, both hardware notes, `architecture.md`, and the shipped code in `yonder-core`, `node-red-dashboard-2-yonder`, `scripts/capture-pages.mjs` and `installer/`.

Three corrections to things you may have been told elsewhere, up front, because they change what you should act on:

- **`revert()` does not reboot.** `packages/yonder-core/src/apply/engine.ts:460-489` writes back the previous config, clears the journal and re-renders. `grep -rn reboot packages/yonder-core/src` finds nothing in the apply path. **`docs/architecture.md:272` says "revert to last-known-good and reboot".** That divergence is real, predates this spec, and deserves its own K-item.
- **The gimbal axis-to-bit mapping is settled**, contrary to §3 line 222. `dji-pocket-2-over-usb.md:199` — "**Bit 0 of the limit byte is pitch; bit 1 is yaw**" — from the sixty-move as-mounted range run at :191-196. The two passages that seeded your hedge (:265-266, :317-318) are earlier runs the as-mounted run superseded and the note never reconciled. The genuinely open bit is **bit 2, presumed roll, never driven to a stop** (:653).
- The palette bug is **K-32**, closed by R-CFG-12; R-CFG-11 is what caused it.

## 1. The six that matter

### 1. §3 imports the gimbal bench's measurements and not its rules

This is the only finding class in the document with a physical consequence on a flying aircraft, and it is the one the review is most confident about.

§3 takes four numbers from `dji-pocket-2-over-usb.md` — rate accuracy, the `0x80` authority flag, ~0.5 s frame validity, the limit flag — and takes none of the note's four **conclusions**. Every one of them bears directly on a gesture §3 presents as settled.

| The note's rule | Where §3 stands |
|---|---|
| ":300-303 **Clamp before sending.** An absolute angle the gimbal cannot reach in yaw is not refused — the controller finds it by pitching over the top… the limit flag is the feedback for the edge, not a substitute for the clamp." Observed as "a mechanical click and a fast spin", asked for four times by the sweep script. | The word `clamp` appears **zero** times in the spec. §2 line 159 gives the discovered range exactly one job: "*Find range* drives a gimbal to its stops **so the dial can draw them**." |
| ":205-209 The daemon "learns the envelope… keeps the four angles it found, and clamps every aim command to them — with the camera's own flag as the backstop **it should never reach**." | §3 line 221: an unfound range means the dial declines to draw an arc. That is a rendering rule. The aim gesture lives on the **picture**, not the dial, so on a fresh install a tap is a positional command with no envelope in existence. |
| ":426-430 "**A gimbal already in trouble must not be commanded at all** — not even recentred. Any limit bit, or a head more than 60° from level, and the session tool refuses everything on command set 4 until the operator has put it right by hand." | §3 lines 213-215 is a pointer changing to the caution tone and an annunciator. No inhibit state exists, so the page keeps accepting aim on a head that is already folded. |
| ":429-433 "`4/0x14` is not understood well enough to use… the only gimbal commands with a clean record are recentre from a sane pose and small yaw moves within the window." ":421-422 "**Incremental mode — bit 0 clear — has no reference to get wrong.**" | §3 line 198 keeps tap-to-point as a settled gesture and never names a wire command for it. |

Two further gaps in the same section:

- **Pitch has no limit model.** §3 says "byte 10 **bit 1** is a limit flag… observed while held against the end of its **yaw** travel", draws one arc, one stop band. The note found pitch's own stop on its own bit at −24.8°, and **no stop at all** in the other pitch direction — "beyond +100° — no flag; the guard's own window ended the search". One arc cannot honestly represent an axis bounded at −25° and open past +100°, and a downward drag reaches a stop with nothing drawn.
- **The half-second fail-safe is the rate frame's property only.** §3 lines 193-196 and §7 line 467 lean the whole link-loss story on expiry. The absolute-angle frame carries a `duration` field (`:412-415` — `0x14` is "arrive in 2 s") and commits: it runs after the link is gone. There is no Stop key anywhere in the design, and the bench's only recovery from a bad move was **powering the camera off by hand**, which is not available in flight — the board is the USB *device*, so there is no port to power-cycle.

**What to do.** In this order:

1. Delete the stale precondition at lines 222-223 and state the mapping, citing `dji-pocket-2-over-usb.md:199`. Fix lines 211 and the Evidence row at 666, which still describe the flag as yaw-only. While there, reconcile `:265-266` and `:317-318` in the note itself, or the next reader imports the same stale hedge.
2. **Say in §3 that the learned envelope is a clamp in the daemon, not a drawing input**, and that every positional command is clamped before it is sent — below both entry points, since R-CAM-16 relays MAVLink commands "on the same terms" and a console-gesture rule leaves that path unguarded.
3. **Rebuild tap-to-point as an incremental offset from the reported attitude** (mode bit 0 clear), or cut it. Incremental is proven to 0.2° over thirty moves and, in the note's words, "has no reference to get wrong". That removes the over-the-top mechanism rather than guarding it. If you cut it, note in §12 that pointing at a spot in frame needs a field of view first — §12 already records that nothing knows what lens is on a camera.
4. **Add a *gimbal inhibited* state**: any limit bit, or a head beyond a stated angle from level, refuses the whole gimbal command set including recentre, drawn in the fault tone. Then decide in this document what the in-flight exit is. The note proves recentre is the wrong answer; `general/0x0b` reboot is untried; the 45 s re-probe (`:453-458`) is a lever but is not proven to clear a stalled head. **If the honest answer is "the aim is lost for the rest of the flight", say so** — it is a design position, and it raises the stakes on the clamp.
5. Two envelopes and two limit bits, drawn and clamped independently, and a *Find range* rule for the case the bench hit: a direction that ends on the **search guard** rather than on the flag is recorded as *no stop found* and drawn open, never given the guard's ceiling as if it were a limit.
6. Put a **Stop** on the rail whenever a gimbal is present, and note in §7 that a positional command already delivered runs to completion after the link goes.

Also missing from §3 entirely, and required by ID: **mode and recentre**. R-CAM-11 names "aim, **mode**, **recentre**, zoom, focus, exposure and white balance"; R-TEL-15 requires the gimbal's *mode* to be shown. Neither word appears in the spec. This is not cosmetic — the note at `:313-315` records that in YawFollow the reachable yaw is about ±60° against a specification pan range of −230° to +70°, "the far side… evidently reachable only in another mode". **A range learned in one mode is wrong by a factor of three in another**, so the stored envelope must be keyed by work mode and a mode change must invalidate the arc.

### 2. §2's "no confirmation countdown" is false against the shipped engine, and its premise is contradicted by §4

Six independent lenses found this. It is the most corroborated finding in the run.

§2 lines 133-137 state as settled fact: *"they do not start a confirmation countdown: `affectsReachability` gates that, and a camera change cannot cost the operator the ability to reach the device."*

`packages/yonder-core/src/apply/reachability.ts:12-17` works the other way round: *"**Everything is reachable until proven otherwise.** This compares the whole document with the parts that cannot affect reachability removed, rather than listing the parts that can. A field added to the schema next year is then treated as load-bearing by default."* `withoutCosmetics()` deletes exactly **three leaves**: `ui.theme`, `remote.zerotier.enabled`, `remote.zerotier.network_id`. A `cameras:` section falls through to load-bearing on its first commit.

So today, as written: every Setup Apply arms the 120 s window on a page that draws neither a countdown nor a confirm control, and the settings quietly revert. That is **K-32 replayed on the camera page**.

The premise is also wrong on the merits, and §4 is the proof. §4 lines 249-251: *"each consumer that leaves over cellular costs its own bitrate. At 2 Mb/s that is 6 Mb/s of uplink for one camera, against a field LTE uplink that is often 1–5."* The console reaches a flying aircraft over that same uplink. An added `outputs[]` entry or a raised ceiling is uplink spend on the path the operator is standing on.

Two consequences the design has not accounted for:

- `BUSY = ["applying","pending","reverting"]` (engine.ts:65). An unconfirmed camera apply **refuses every other apply for two minutes** — a network change included. On an aircraft that is the wrong thing to be locked out of.
- The expiry re-runs `renderAll(previous)` across every renderer, so a camera timeout re-runs `enableWifiRadio`, an `nmcli` device list and an `addOrModify` per owned profile. `settleRadio` skips steps already in the wanted state, so nothing bounces — but it is work a camera apply should not cause.

**What to do.** Withdraw the blanket claim and split the exemption at leaf granularity, the way `reachability.ts` demands and R-VPN-07 requires — *"earned by measurement rather than by argument"*.

- Exempt, named leaf by leaf: `cameras[].controls.*`, `resolution`, `framerate`, `codec`, and the preview's size and rate.
- **Keep the window on `outputs[]` and on bitrate**, because both are egress on the shared path. Say plainly that nobody has yet measured what a saturated uplink does to a console session on a board, so those stay load-bearing until somebody does.
- Add the work to §11: `withoutCosmetics()` has no mechanism for a repeated structure at all — it deletes named leaves off a fixed object shape, and the file explicitly refuses subtree exemptions. An array-aware exemption is **new code with its own tests**, and a test that a newly added camera field falls through to load-bearing.
- Then redesign the Setup deck for whichever answer wins. §2's conclusion — no camera control carries the irreversible tone, Apply is the single primary action in the select tone — is downstream of this and does not survive it unchanged.
- Add a cross-field refusal: `outputs[].port` may not equal `ui.port`. Today's two port-carrying output types (`rtp`, `srt`) are UDP against a TCP console, so nothing collides yet — but a bind race after a reboot is a config that is confirmed while it looks fine and bites on the next boot, which is precisely the class a window never catches.

### 3. Nothing says who may reach the media server — and M4's exit criterion is "from another network"

`auth`, `password`, `credential`, `R-SEC` and `mediamtx` appear **zero** times in 678 lines (the two `auth` hits are "control-authority"). The pipeline this design inherits (`architecture.md:130-136`) terminates in mediamtx on its own ports. The console's gate is `httpNodeAuth` at `httpNodeRoot` (`console/settings.ts:251`); the editor has its own `adminAuth`. **Neither is in the path of a request to the media server.**

M4's exit criterion (`roadmap.md:251-253`) is *"usable video in a browser, over a cellular link, from another network — while a ground station receives the same feed."* From another network means over the mesh, which M2a proved reaches the console from a machine sharing no local network. So the milestone is not done until the picture is watchable over the overlay — and as designed, watchable by anyone on it, with no login.

I would not frame this as "put the video behind the console password". R-VID-01 (P1) sends RTP/UDP to Mission Planner and QGC, which cannot hold a console session. The deliverable is a **stated per-listener posture**:

- Which mediamtx listeners are enabled at all. RTMP and HLS are in ADR-0003's list and unused by this design — they should be **off**, not merely unmentioned.
- Which bind loopback-only.
- What stands in front of the ones that do not.
- **The load-bearing question: is the browser's WebRTC proxied through the console's authenticated origin, or does it reach mediamtx directly?** That alone decides whether the console password protects the picture, and the spec does not say.

This needs a section in the spec and an R-SEC requirement to carry it, in the same change — R-SEC-04 is write-only, so there is no requirement covering a read path today. The gap is inherited from `architecture.md` §3.2 and ADR-0003, not created here; this design is simply the moment it has to be settled.

### 4. The uplink budget instrument has no capacity source in M4, and the cap under-counts

Three separate problems in §4, all in the same paragraph block.

**(a) The mark has no source.** §4 line 284 draws "a mark at the **measured** capacity". Nothing in M4 measures uplink capacity. R-DIA-03 is M7 (`roadmap.md:324`); R-VID-07's running estimate is M9 (`:352`); R-NET-10's throughput gives consumption, not capacity. The composed pipeline cannot produce one either — the ground-station branch is `rtph264pay ! udpsink` with no `rtpbin` (`usb-camera-on-a-pi-4.md:226`), so there is no RTCP session at all. **R-VID-11 is P1 in M4** and its capacity term has no producer before M7.

**(b) The cap ignores how many consumers carry the main encode.** Line 273 caps the adaptive ceiling at (uplink − preview), when §4's own evidence 22 lines earlier is that each remote consumer costs the full bitrate again. The held full-rate soft key at line 269 makes N=2 inside M4. State it as `(uplink − preview − other non-main-encode egress) ÷ N`, and say what happens when N rises in flight — a second RTSP puller either lowers everyone's ceiling or is refused. R-CAM-10 covers refusing a *configuration*, not a runtime connection, so this needs its own sentence.

**(c) Only video is on the track.** MAVLink to up to three ground stations (R-MAV-03) and the mesh keepalives that are the only route to a board behind CGNAT are uncounted, so the green zone runs to 100% of the modem. An operator doing exactly what §4 and §5 train — fill to the mark — leaves nothing for telemetry.

**What to do.** Say in §4 what the M4 mark reads: an **operator-declared uplink figure** carried in config, becoming a measured moving bound when R-DIA-03 or R-VID-07 lands. Draw a **labelled leading reserve band** for telemetry, the interface's own session and the mesh, with the video mark at capacity *less* that band — an unlabelled 700 kb/s that is spoken for reads as a broken instrument. Fix the divisor. Then either amend R-VID-11 ("…less what that path must keep for telemetry and the mesh, shown as a reserved share of the same track") or pull R-DIA-03 into M4 in the roadmap.

The cheapest honest source is in the repo already and is worth taking: **`srtsink`'s `stats` structure** reports RTT, send-buffer level, loss and an estimated link bandwidth. That settles `architecture.md` open question 3 with an answer instead of another deferral. Where SRT is not the transport, `/sys/class/net/wwan0/statistics/tx_bytes` sampled against the sum of encoder targets works today and costs nothing.

### 5. The degrade model covers one failure of three, and misses the one M4 exists to provoke

§6 names three causes of no picture — a blocked browser, a carrier discarding UDP, a camera that stopped producing frames — and §7 designs the entire holding treatment for a fourth thing: the console losing its own link. Two states fall between them.

**A media path that dies while the console session is healthy.** This is the commonest cellular failure. §6's twelve-second timer never starts, because the stream did establish (line 410: "fails to establish", and R-VID-14 at requirements.md:122 carries the same establishment-only scoping). §7 does not fire either. So the page most likely holds a frozen frame labelled Live — the exact hazard §7 was written to prevent. Widen the trigger to "fails to establish, **or stops arriving**", and widen R-VID-14's wording in the same change or §6 and §9 disagree.

**A board that is throttling or browning out.** K-41 records the board doing this, and R-SYS-09 was put into M4 with the stated reason that *"encoding video is what pushes the draw up, so M4 is the milestone that provokes the fault it needs to report."* This design starts two encoders per camera. But a throttled board does not present as loss of contact — it presents as a session that stays up and delivers fewer frames. Every one of §7's four staleness signals is keyed to contact being lost, and §6's three settings are Live/Stills/Off. The spec's own Evidence table concedes the thermal figure was "never throttled" in **open air** and that "sustained encode in a closed airframe is untested". So the page that causes the fault has nowhere to report it.

**What to do.** Add a fourth entry to §6's cause list — the board's own pipeline or encoder stopped, distinct from the camera — and put the readout strip's *delivered* frame rate against the *commanded* rate, with a divergence drawn in the caution tone. Put board-side degrade (R-SYS-09's throttle/undervoltage state) on the Cameras page beside the shared budget, following your own rule at §5 line 338 that shared facts live there; surface its per-camera consequence in §6's reason line. And make §7's reconnect bullet **name the reboot** rather than absorbing it — as written, a board that restarted is indistinguishable from a link that merely came back.

Three silences with three different operator responses (wait / act / land) should not share one treatment.

### 6. There is no runtime control surface, and R-CTL-01 (P1, M4) falls into the hole

§11's four components are all pure or start-time: capability model, probing, pipeline **composition** ("a value that can be asserted in a test"), receive-line renderer. Nothing owns a handle on a running pipeline, and `architecture.md:162` describes GStreamer as "spawn / supervise" — a subprocess with no property, event or ioctl access.

Two things in the design need one and cannot get it from a shell-launched `gst-launch` line:

- **A keyframe forced on subscribe** (§7 line 460, R-VID-09). mediamtx cannot ask a GStreamer publisher for an IDR; ADR-0003 gives no such API, and nothing has ever been published to mediamtx on this board.
- **A bitrate that changes without dropping the picture.** R-VID-07's adaptive loop — which §4 leans on hard — cannot restart the pipeline on every adaptation. Note that **bitrate appears in neither example column of §2's control table**, so the page currently cannot say which of the three legends it carries. That is the sharpest symptom.

Separately, **R-CTL-01 — "Start and stop each stream independently", P1, `roadmap.md:234`, M4 — is never cited anywhere in the spec**, and the page has no control for it. §6's Off is the browser's own view and explicitly changes nothing on the aircraft. §5 line 315 already assumes a camera can be "not streaming at all" with no way to get there. An operator watching the budget track go over the mark — which §4 and §5 exist to make them do — has nothing on the rail to do about it.

**What to do.** Add a fifth §11 component: a supervised pipeline that exposes at minimum force-keyframe, set-bitrate and per-camera run state, and say whether that is an in-process GStreamer application with an RPC socket or something narrower. Add a per-camera run state to §2, cite R-CTL-01, and say whether stopping is a config write or a runtime action. Then re-classify each control in §2's table by whether that channel can serve it live.

A cheaper partial answer worth taking regardless: **give the preview branch its own short GOP (~1 s) and keep the long GOP on the ground-station branch.** §4 has already made the two branches separate encoder instances, so a keyframe on reconnect costs a few tens of kb/s on a branch running at a few hundred, with no control-plane round trip and **no mediamtx feature dependency at all**. That turns the design's one untested external dependency into an optimisation on the path where a grey rectangle is actually seen by a person.

## 2. Everything else confirmed

### The capability model (§1, §11)

- **No settability axis.** "One type, three states" answers *is it there*, not *may the operator set it now*. The measured case: `dji-pocket-2-over-usb.md:508-509` — ISO is manual-only and inert while exposure mode is Program, and §2 line 124 lists exposure as an *applies live* control for that exact camera. V4L2's `INACTIVE` flag is the same shape on the UVC side. Minimum fix: one axis, `settable | governedBy: CapabilityId | readOnly{why}`, so the deck greys the control and **names what owns it** rather than leaving a permanent unexplained divergence mark. The spec also has unclassified read-only capabilities already — §2 line 154's encoder and decoder.
- **No mode axis.** §8 commits the model to mapping onto `CAMERA_INFORMATION` "without a translation layer", and that flagset carries `HAS_MODES` plus the two cross-mode capture flags. The accessory camera has a **proven** video/photo working mode (`camera/0x10`, state push toggles), and §6 promises "the camera's own photo where it has one" for a device whose photo command (`camera/0x01`) is untried and may require leaving video mode — which is the live view the operator is aiming with. Either add `validInModes`, or at minimum give the capture capabilities the two booleans the flagset needs, and say in §6 what a snapshot does to the picture on a mode-switching camera.
- **The model is in a package the Vue half cannot depend on.** §11 puts the capability model in `node-red-contrib-yonder-video` and the facts row in `node-red-dashboard-2-yonder`. The dependency graph is a strict star — every sibling declares `{"yonder-core":"^0.1.0"}` and nothing else — and no contrib package ships a browser-safe subpath export. As written the model is duplicated or you grow the repo's first contrib→contrib edge. **`yonder-core/presentation` exists for exactly this** (`console/presentation.ts:16-23`). Split it: the capability *type* and the three-state decision go there beside `reading()`; the *probing* stays in the video package. Note that three packages must agree, not two — R-VID-12/R-CAM-16 put the same model in `node-red-contrib-yonder-mavlink`. The MAVLink flagset mapping itself should stay device-side; a Vue component never touches those bits, and `presentation.ts`'s third admission rule is "something both a node and a component legitimately need to agree about".
- **A camera page hidden by R-UI-03 is still reachable by URL.** Dashboard registers a route per `ui-page` regardless of visibility; only the nav filters. One line in §5: a deep link to a slot holding no camera states that fact, with when it was last seen.
- **Port identity has no inverse.** R-CAM-05 anchors identity to the port so a camera survives a reboot. Nothing says what makes a port's stored identity stale when a *different* camera appears there — it inherits the name, the resolution, the outputs. Either a paragraph in §2 (changed vendor/product/serial at a known topology path marks the stored configuration stale, and the parts the new device cannot satisfy are stated rather than coerced) or an entry in §12 beside the re-enumeration bullet.

### Interaction (§3, §4, §11)

- **Neither the aim rate nor tap-to-point is scaled by the receiver-side zoom crop.** The accessory camera's zoom is a crop at the receiver, up to 10× (`:555`). Unscaled, the same finger travel moves the scene ten times as fast at maximum zoom — the oscillation the rate model was chosen to prevent, at the magnification where the operator is least tolerant of it. **The receiver knows the crop factor exactly**, so dividing commanded rate by it is buildable today. One sentence in §3, scoped to the picture gesture — the dial's arc is real angle and is not scaled.
- **The dial carries a rate and an angle on one face.** §3 lines 204-206 put "the commanded rate where you are pushing" and "the reported attitude where the camera actually is" on one self-centring control, then give that control a travel arc with stop bands, which is an angle scale. With the head at 40° yaw the attitude mark sits at 40° while the control rests centred, and the gap the design says "makes the lag legible" is a rate minus an angle. Name the dial's scale and unit, say what centre is, say what the gap means, and say **which mark takes the caution tone** at a stop — "the pointer" names one pointer on a dial with two. (The word *pointer* is also used for the input device at line 228 and a dial mark at line 214. Pick one sense.)
- **Nothing arbitrates the picture's drag against page scrolling.** The deck wraps to one column on a phone, so scrolling is routine, and the picture is the aim surface. This bites from M5 and bites hardest in the **compact Cockpit form**, where §2 line 99 drops the deck — so the picture is the *only* aim surface and "let the page scroll, aim on the dial" is not available. Settle it in the pointer primitive.
- **Tap and drag share one pointer with no discriminator.** Every tap begins as a zero-length drag. State the threshold in movement and time, and say what a press that starts and returns inside it does. Also decide whether leaving a camera mid-slew (a strip tap, a deep link, a backgrounded tab) sends an explicit zero-rate or relies on the ~0.5 s expiry — expiry is the right default, but half a second of unattended slew should be a stated cost.
- **"On reconnection no pointer is down" is a rule the widget enforces, not a fact about the world.** The session reconnects unattended; holding and watching for the picture to come back is the natural behaviour. State that a pointer held across a reconnection does not re-arm and aiming restarts only on a fresh press, and say what the dial draws while it is deflected but commanded centred.
- **Nothing is specified for the twelve seconds before the stills fallback fires.** On a first connection at a field there is no previous frame to hold, and this is the most common first twelve seconds any operator spends on the page. Your own rule at §5 line 314 — "a tile is never blank without saying why" — is stated for a 100 px thumbnail and not for the largest region on the page.
- **The held full-rate key's switch cost is never named.** It is a fresh WHEP negotiation on a different mediamtx path — offer, ICE, DTLS, then a decodable frame, on a link the spec assumes has ~300 ms of lag. As specified the operator gets a gap where the picture was and the full-rate frame arriving about when the finger lifts. Decide: hold the preview until the new stream yields its first frame, plus a minimum dwell.

### State machine

- **A stream restart or a Setup Apply while the board is recording is undefined.** Board-side recording is taken from the running pipeline and stream controls rebuild it. Refuse the change, or close the file cleanly and start a new one — either is defensible, silence is not. The asymmetry is the point: the same control has two different consequences depending on whether the camera or the board holds the file. Note also that the "Recording" row's trigger — "the camera's own status push" — is written only for a camera with its own recorder and does not describe the board-side case R-CAM-17 requires.
- **The four recording states do not distinguish the two recording paths.** "No medium · fault tone · Refused" is reachable on the board's own card, where the board knows its storage and its R-STO-06 reserve. It is **not reachable on the accessory camera**, which by §6's own line 368 acknowledges a record command with no card in it. There, the no-card case lands in "Sent, not confirmed" and nothing bounds how long it sits there — an operator presses Record before takeoff, sees amber, and flies believing a recording may be running. Give that state a dwell limit after which it becomes fault, and say which path each row's trigger belongs to.
- **A reboot mid-recording has no state and no file-integrity decision**, on a board K-41 says reboots by itself. Nothing says the recording is written so a power loss costs the tail rather than the file, and §7's "Coming back" lists three things that restart and one that does not — a recording that was running is in neither list, so the annunciator resolves silently to "Not recording". `the board restarted; the recording ended at N minutes` is a fact the journal and the file's own length can both supply.
- **Periodic stills have no named producer and no server.** §6 defers to "the same mechanism that draws the strip" and §5 never names it either. mediamtx offers WebRTC, RTSP, SRT, RTMP and HLS — all continuous transports, **no image endpoint** — and the stills path must work in exactly the cases the media transports do not, so it must be a different transport served by a different component. Add one bullet to §11: where frames are tapped per source kind, what holds the latest frame and timestamp per camera (§5 line 313 already assumes this exists), and what serves them over the console's own channel.

### Verification and CI

- **§10's mechanism is wrong for M4, and the consequence is worse than a skip.** `scripts/capture-pages.mjs:41-51` builds its page list by walking `ui-page` nodes in the **checked-in `flows/flows.json`** — it has no knowledge of hardware. So the silent-non-coverage hazard §10 describes begins at **M6, with R-UI-03**. In M4 and M5 the camera page sits in the committed flows and the gate visits it on a camera-less runner: either it fails on a browser error / `requestfailed` when the picture pane negotiates WebRTC with no media server, or — worse — it records the camera-less shape into `docs/console/shape` as the reviewed baseline. **The gate is not blind, it is confidently photographing the wrong page.** The fixture must land *before or with* the first camera `ui-page` node.
- **A fixture is not enough — the page is generated, so one fixture photographs one shape.** Name a profile set (fixed UVC; gimballed accessory; advertised-not-answered; range-unknown; no-medium; rejected) and derive the coverage assertion from the capability **type** rather than a hand-written list. `console/theme.test.ts:9-17` already records why: "A hand-written list of the things under test is a list that goes stale without failing."
- **The shape gate is not enforced on the platform CI runs on.** `docs/console/shape/` holds only `.darwin.json`. On Linux, `capture-pages.mjs` takes the `accept || !existsSync(refPath)` branch, writes a `.linux.json`, prints `new`, increments neither `failures` nor `changed` — and `git diff --exit-code` does not report untracked files. So the **shape half of R-UI-12 is recorded on every CI run and enforced on none.** Fourteen unenforced references is a quiet gap; camera profiles × deck × palette makes it a directory nobody trusts. Fix before the camera pages multiply it.
- **Determinism seams the picture pane needs.** The synthetic camera must emit a byte-identical pattern with no burned-in timecode, or `.y-picture` joins the `LIVE` mask list and the gate paints over the exact surface this design is about. And the clock must be injectable at the package boundary: the 12 s fallback, the ~0.5 s expiry, elapsed recording time, last-frame age and §7's one-minute degrade progression are all load-bearing and none is capturable against a 400 ms settle without a fake clock.
- **The two riskiest primitives cannot be proven by `--press`**, which performs one `click()`. Drive them with `mouse.down/move/up` and touch emulation, and test the endings that are not clean: `pointerup` outside bounds, **`pointercancel`** (the browser stealing the gesture for a scroll — the case a hand-written test never includes and a real tablet produces constantly), `visibilitychange` while held, a second pointer landing, the socket closing while held. - **The theme test's tone list is hand-written** (`theme.test.ts:91-100` loops `["good","waiting","bad","select"]`), so the new `recording` token will silently skip the legibility check. Derive both loops from the palette, and add a **pairwise separation** assertion with an explicit exception table — nothing today checks that a red `recording` can be told from a red-ish `bad`, which is the entire argument §6 makes for the token.

Three of §6's four states already map onto existing command states (`idle`, `pending`, `rejected`).

## 5. What this review is not confident about

- **Whether browser signalling terminates at the console or at mediamtx.** The spec does not say, and it is the single fact that decides whether the console password protects the picture. Settled by one sentence from you, not by more reading.
